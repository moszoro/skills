# The "David Explains" Reel Formula — reverse-engineered from 8 reels

Reverse-engineered from 8 transcribed reels (4.5k–90k likes). Every reel is the same
machine: **take one familiar behavior, tell the viewer the obvious reason is wrong, then
walk them down a chain of escalating examples to a hidden "real reason," and close on a
quotable law + a hook into the next video.** Below is the structure, the retention
engineering, and a fill-in-the-blank template you can drop any topic into.

---

## The 7-beat structure (with time budget for a 60–120s reel)

| # | Beat | % of runtime | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold-open paradox** | 0–8s | Open a loop the brain *needs* closed |
| 2 | **"Neither / not the real reason" reframe** | 8–18s | Kill the obvious answer, promise a hidden one |
| 3 | **Name-drop + coin a term** | ~15–30s | Borrow authority, hand over a shareable label |
| 4 | **Escalating concrete examples** | bulk | Prove it with vivid, specific, rising cases |
| 5 | **Mid-video re-hook (the twist)** | ~60–70% | Re-open attention exactly where people drop |
| 6 | **Callback + quotable payoff** | last 15–20s | Resolve beat 1, compress into one law |
| 7 | **Cliffhanger + comment bait** | last 5s + caption | Tee up the next video, ask a question |

---

## Beat-by-beat, with the exact moves they use

### 1 — Cold-open paradox (0–8s) — the single most important line
No intro, no "hey guys." First sentence is a **paradox, myth, or impossible-sounding fact**
framed as a question or a flat claim. Pick one of these five hook molds (all pulled from the reels).
**Full hook reference (the 5 molds, real examples, shared DNA, anti-patterns, how to build ours): `HOOKS.md`.**

- **Paradox question:** "How is it you don't feel cheated paying $15 for water at a gala, but would storm out of a roadside store if charged the same?"
- **Triple negation → question:** "Nobody buys a Rolex to tell the time, nobody buys a Birkin to carry things, nobody buys a Ferrari to get from A to B. So what are they actually buying?"
- **Myth-bust:** "Most people argue a woman gets ready for the male gaze. Others say the female gaze. Neither is correct."
- **Surprising fact:** "The Marlboro Man is the most masculine icon in advertising. But the brand was originally built for women."
- **Bold claim:** "The reason your customers give for buying your product is rarely the real reason they bought it."

Rule: the hook must name a behavior the viewer **already does or recognizes**, then make it
suddenly feel irrational. That gap = the open loop.

### 2 — Reframe (8–18s)
Explicitly invalidate the obvious explanation and promise a hidden mechanism:
"the real reason goes deeper than he realizes," "Neither is correct, because both assume…,"
"who we are constantly changes with our environment." This is the **promise** that keeps them.

### 3 — Authority + coined term
Drop **1–2 credible names** and **hand over a labeled concept**: Rory Sutherland / *Alchemy* →
"psychologic"; Jonathan Haidt → "self-placebo"; Veblen → "conspicuous consumption"; Nicholas
Humphrey → the "placebo-cost rule." The name buys trust; the coined term is what viewers
repeat in comments and to friends (built-in shareability).

**Frame it in first person — this is what we kept missing.** He does not say "experts say." He
makes the idea feel like something *he* worked out and is handing you. Two framings, usually stacked:
- **His own conclusion:** "After much pondering, **I concluded that**…", "**In my last video I introduced** a concept called psychologic", "the way I see it…", "then **I found the name for it**."
- **The borrowed coining, relayed:** "**He called it** conspicuous consumption", "Rory Sutherland **points to** this in Alchemy."
"I realized / I concluded / here's the name I found for it" turns a cited fact into a personal
discovery, which pulls far harder than an impersonal "there's a term for this." Then hand over the label.

### 4 — Escalating concrete examples (the body)
Never abstract for long. A **new concrete image every ~10–15s**, rising in stakes.

**Make every example a scene you can feel — the vividness standard.** A named example is not a
summary, it is a SCENE: the objects, the numbers, the exact words, the room, put in present tense
so the viewer pictures it and *feels* it. This is the single biggest quality lever, and the easiest
to under-do.
- Flat (what to avoid): "During a war, Prussia asked women to give gold for iron jewellery."
- David: "Hand over your gold jewellery. Your necklaces, your bracelets, your rings. In return, iron replicas, stamped with four words: I gave gold for iron. Women started showing up to social gatherings wearing the iron."
- Put the viewer *inside* it, second person present tense: "Imagine being seen arguing with the cashier about a four dollar markup."
Rule of thumb: if you can't see the light, the hands, and one exact detail (a number, a stamped
phrase, the time on the clock), expand it until you can. Payoffs stay crisp; **examples get vivid.**

Their tools:
- **Contrast pairs:** $15k Rolex vs $20 Casio; Birkin vs $15 tote; Ferrari vs Porsche/Toyota.
- **Price/number ladders:** water — home free → park $5 → concert $10 → gala $15.
- **Immersive 2nd-person scenario, present tense:** "you're in the stadium, your country scores… VAR intervenes… offside… a brutal crushing plummet."
- **Mini historical arc as story:** Prussia 1813 gold-for-iron; Marlboro's 30-year rise.
- **Hard specifics:** $900 toolbox, 1000% popcorn markup, "less than 10% of Americans brushed."

### 5 — Mid-video re-hook (the twist) at ~60–70%
Right where viewers bail, they drop a **counterintuitive reversal** or "here's where it gets
interesting": recessions make men buy *more* tools, not fewer; "millions of men chose the
cancer"; the makeup "maintenance loop." This is the second engine that carries the back half.

### 6 — Callback + quotable payoff (last 15–20s)
**Return to the exact opening image** ("Now we return to the mystery of the two water bottles")
and resolve it in **one compressed, tweetable law**:
- "We are fluid, shape-shifting organisms who don't feel cheated when we overpay, as long as the price fits the identity of the environment we're in."
- "The tobacco never changed. Mass adoption came from rewriting what the object meant."
The payoff must feel like *you just leveled up* — that's what earns the like/share.

### 7 — Cliffhanger + comment bait (last 5s + caption)
Most reels **tee up the next one**: "Next, we go inward — the same psychology pointing the
opposite direction," or literally end on an unresolved question the next video answers. The
**caption asks for an opinion** ("What's your view on this?") to farm comments.

## The retention engineering (why you stay to the end)

1. **Open-loop cold open** — a question with no answer yet. You physically can't leave it.
2. **Delayed payoff** — the resolving line is withheld until the final seconds. To get the dopamine of "closing the loop," you have to watch it all.
3. **Callback structure** — beat 6 pays off beat 1, so the ending only makes sense if you saw the start (rewards full-watch, punishes skipping).
4. **A fresh concrete image every 10–15s** — no dead air, no abstract drift. The eye/ear always has a new specific thing.
5. **Mid-video re-hook** — a second surprise placed at the 60–70% drop-off cliff.
6. **Second person + present tense** — "your brain," "you're in the stadium" — it's about *you*, happening *now*.
7. **One idea per reel** — never two concepts. Tight = re-watchable = higher completion.

## The channel-level loop (why they binge the whole account)
Each reel is **serialized**: "In my last video…" + "…that is next." This turns 8 separate
reels into one chain, so a hooked viewer autoplays down the whole profile. The retention
isn't just per-video — it's engineered across the feed.

## What the numbers say (8-reel sample)
- **Top performers (90k / 73k / 72k):** universal subject (water, luxury, football) + a razor hook (paradox / triple-negation / contrarian) + a **self-contained payoff**.
- **Lowest (4.5k / 7.8k):** the 4.5k reel *ends on an open question with no payoff* (pure setup for the next video) → less shareable; the 7.8k was the **series opener** before the audience compounded.
- Takeaway: **every reel needs its own standalone payoff** even while it hooks the next. Don't ship a pure cliffhanger.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — same structure, totally different topic
**Topic: "Why your best ideas come in the shower."** (proves the formula transfers)

- **[0:00] Hook (paradox):** "You can sit at your desk for three hours and get nothing — then solve the whole problem in the shower in ninety seconds. Why does your brain wait until you're wet and holding shampoo?"
- **[0:09] Reframe:** "It's not the water, and it's not luck. Your best thinking is *designed* to happen when you stop trying."
- **[0:16] Authority + term:** "Neuroscientists call it the *default mode network* — the circuit that only switches on when your focused attention switches off."
- **[0:25] Escalating examples:** desk = spotlight, narrow and tense · shower = low-stakes, warm, autopilot, mild dopamine · the mind wanders and starts connecting distant ideas · (numbers) most reported 'aha' moments happen away from the desk — walking, showering, falling asleep · 2nd-person scene: "you rinse, your mind drifts to lunch, and mid-thought the answer just… arrives."
- **[~1:05] Mid re-hook (twist):** "Here's the strange part — the harder you *try* to force the idea, the more you jam the exact network that produces it. Effort is the block."
- **[last 0:20] Callback + payoff:** "So back to the shower. The idea didn't come because you relaxed — it came because you finally got out of your own way. Your breakthroughs aren't waiting on more focus. They're waiting on less."
- **[last 0:05] Cliffhanger + caption:** spoken: "And there's a way to trigger this on purpose, without the shower — that's next." · caption: "Your best ideas come when you stop chasing them. Where do yours hit you?"

## Pre-publish checklist
- [ ] First line is a paradox/myth/impossible fact — no intro.
- [ ] Obvious reason is explicitly killed by ~0:15.
- [ ] Exactly one idea. One coined term.
- [ ] A new concrete image at least every ~15s. Zero abstract stretches.
- [ ] Every example is a SCENE you can feel (objects, numbers, exact words, present tense), not a summary.
- [ ] The coined term is handed over in first person ("I concluded / here's the name I found for it"), not "experts say".
- [ ] A twist/re-hook at ~65% of runtime.
- [ ] Ending calls back to the opening image and lands one quotable line.
- [ ] It has a standalone payoff (not a pure cliffhanger) AND teases the next video.
- [ ] Caption restates the law in one line and asks a question.
